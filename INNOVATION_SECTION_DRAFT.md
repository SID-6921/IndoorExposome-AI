## The core innovation is the event-level reframing, not the underlying model

It would be easy to overstate the novelty of the machine-learning method itself — logistic regression is not new, and even the stronger baselines tested (Random Forest, XGBoost) are standard, off-the-shelf approaches. The genuine innovation demonstrated in this preliminary work is **how the prediction problem is framed and evaluated**, and there is direct evidence — from this project's own data, not just assertion — that the framing is where the real information lives, independent of model sophistication.

The same underlying model, evaluated two different ways, produced two different conclusions:

- Evaluated with conventional machine-learning metrics on a per-minute classification basis (AUPRC, AUROC, raw false-positive rate), the preliminary models did **not** clearly or consistently outperform a naive persistence baseline (`STEP2_PRELIMINARY_BASELINE_RESULTS.md`) — the kind of result that would typically be read as a negative or inconclusive finding, and is in fact how most published indoor-air-quality forecasting work reports results.
- Evaluated instead at the **event level** — consolidating repeated positive predictions into discrete alert events with a cooldown period, reporting first-actionable-alert lead time rather than average lead time across all flagged windows, and fixing the false-alarm *rate* (alerts/property-day) as the operating constraint rather than optimizing for a statistical metric like F1 — the identical model cleared a defensible operational benchmark (≥50% episode detection at ≤2 false alerts/property-day with a clinically/operationally meaningful lead time) (`STEP2B_EVENT_LEVEL_CALIBRATION_ANALYSIS.md`).

This gap is the central methodological claim of the Innovation section: **conventional per-minute classification metrics do not answer the question that determines whether a product is usable — "how many real alerts, at what false-alarm cost, with how much real warning" — and reframing the evaluation around that question surfaces value that standard reporting conventions miss.** This reframing, and its accompanying operational-benchmark methodology (fixed alert-burden operating points, home-level cluster-bootstrap confidence intervals, pre-registered frozen-model holdout evaluation before any confirmatory test), is the transferable contribution — applicable to any sustained-exposure-episode forecasting problem, not specific to PM2.5 or to this model family.

## Positioning relative to existing products (see also `LITERATURE_AND_MARKET_NOTES.md`)

A market scan of major consumer/commercial indoor air quality products (Awair, Airthings, Dyson, PurpleAir, IQAir) found that current products are uniformly reactive: they display current readings and/or fire an alert once a threshold is already crossed, rather than forecasting an episode before it occurs.

**IQAir/AirVisual is the one product that must be positioned carefully rather than either overclaimed against or lumped in with the reactive products.** IQAir advertises "predictive" air-quality forecasts, but per their own documentation, this is a 3–7-day *outdoor*, *city/regional-scale* forecast built from numerical weather prediction models and government/community monitoring stations — fundamentally a different prediction target (ambient outdoor conditions days out, at city resolution) from what this system targets (a specific room's indoor PM2.5 trajectory, 30–60 minutes out, personalized to that space's own sensor history and context). The Innovation section should state this distinction explicitly: **there is a partial conceptual overlap in that both systems use the word "predictive," but no identified product performs personalized, room-level, near-term (sub-hour) indoor exposure forecasting** — which is the actual claim being made here, and should be stated precisely enough that a reviewer familiar with IQAir's marketing cannot reasonably characterize it as an overclaim.

## Secondary methodological contributions worth noting

- **Cross-cohort external validation under real domain shift.** Validating across two independently collected cohorts with different sensors, countries, climates, and native time resolutions (1-minute vs. hourly) required an explicit, documented methodology for resolution-matched feature construction (aggregating the higher-resolution cohort to match the lower-resolution one, rather than the reverse) and honest handling of features that don't exist in both cohorts (e.g., excluding a feature group from the trained model rather than force-fitting it, see `FROZEN_CONFIG_PORTABLE.md`) — a transferable template for any indoor-environment-sensing product that must eventually generalize across heterogeneous hardware deployments.
- **Pre-registration discipline applied to a preliminary/exploratory setting.** The frozen-model-before-holdout protocol (`FROZEN_CONFIG.md`, `STEP2C_HOLDOUT_RESULTS.md`) and the explicit "one clean exploratory run, report as-is" discipline applied to speculative directions (hybrid definitions, regime-stratified models) demonstrate a level of methodological rigor uncommon in preliminary/Phase I data packages, where the temptation to iterate against a test set until results look favorable is high.
